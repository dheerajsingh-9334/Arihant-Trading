01

The business & the problem

Arihant sells security & defence equipment to the government — hand-held and door-frame metal detectors, X-ray baggage scanners, thermal imagers, night-vision monoculars, bomb blankets, crash-rated bollards and boom barriers.

Buyers are BSF, the Indian Army, Railways, Nuclear Power Corporation and Police, and almost every order runs through GeM tenders — the GEM/2026/B/… numbers in the tender sheet. Today the whole operation lives on WhatsApp, Excel and personal follow-ups, and no one at the top can see who is doing what. That is the pain the BOS removes.

What you solve, and what Arihant gets

Today's problemWhat the BOS gives them
Tender info scattered across WhatsApp & portals; deadlines missedOne tender pipeline with EMD, bid-closing dates, approvals and win/loss reasons. A single missed date is a lost government order worth lakhs.
Nobody knows which demo unit is freeCentral equipment availability across Patna / Delhi / Kolkata
Demos happen, but no one learns why they failStructured demo outcome & failure-reason analysis
Staff recreate their whole day at night for reporting"Enter once, use everywhere" — daily work becomes the report automatically
Follow-ups fall through the cracksAutomatic reminders + clear ownership and accountability

The one line to say to the client: "A single system where every lead, tender, demo, proposal, service ticket, visit and expense lives in one place — so management sees, in real time, what's pending, what's delayed and who's responsible, without chasing anyone on WhatsApp."

Scope reality & phasing

All 16 modules at production quality in 8 days, solo, is not realistic — the blueprint is a multi-month build for a small team. The winning move is to ship a real, deployed pilot of the highest-value modules, loaded with Arihant's own data, and phase the rest.

The blueprint plans this for you: Section 49 (Phase 1 Scope), Section 53 (Stage 1–9 sequence) and Section 51 (Scope Freeze). Use its own words to frame the split.

PHASE 1 · THE 8-DAY PILOT
Ship & deploy these five

1. Auth + Roles + User management
2. Leads & Customers + Interaction timeline
3. Tender management (the demo "wow")
4. Visit / field planning
5. Management dashboard (the exception view)

PHASE 2 · SCHEMA ALREADY BUILT
Screens on tables that exist

Demo & equipment · Proposals · Service tickets · Expenses & approval · Tasks & blockers · full Notifications · Audit trail. The database already contains every one of these tables, so nothing gets re-architected — you only add UI.

The email that protects your ₹10L: get written sign-off on this Phase-1 split before you write code (Section 51 literally asks the client to do this). That single message is the difference between "delivered Phase 1 on time" and "failed to deliver 16 modules."

03

Architecture & stack

NestJS backend, Next.js frontend, Supabase (Postgres + Auth + Storage), no Prisma. For 300 users this stack is effortless — don't spend a minute on scale.

The decisions that matter

ConcernChoice & why
DB access (no Prisma)Kysely — type-safe SQL query builder for Postgres. ORM-grade autocomplete, but you write real SQL, no migration engine fighting you. Generate types with kysely-codegen.
AuthSupabase Auth issues logins + JWTs. NestJS verifies the JWT in a guard with the Supabase JWT secret, loads role from users, enforces RBAC.
AuthorizationNestJS is the only backend and uses the service_role key (bypasses RLS), so enforce permissions in NestJS guards, not RLS. Region scoping = where region_id = :userRegion. Keep RLS off while building; enable as defence-in-depth before handover.
FilesSupabase Storage for receipts & service reports; store the path in attachments.
DeploymentFrontend → Vercel, NestJS → Railway/Render, DB hosted on Supabase. Deploy the empty apps on Day 0 so every later push auto-deploys.

Design decisions grounded in the sheets

  • organisations is the dedupe anchor. Contacts, leads, tenders and visits all hang off it — that's how "fresh vs re-approached lead" (§7) works: never duplicate a customer, just add a new interaction to the same org.
  • leads grain = organisation + product, matching each row of your Funnel and Bill&Book sheets. Bill&Book is folded in via booking_month / billing_month / order_status, so it's one clean pipeline.
  • interactions is the unified customer timeline — every call/visit/demo/proposal logs here for "one chronological history."
  • tenders mirrors your North Tender Sheet columns (tender no, zone, bid-closing date with time, EMD, corrigendum), plus tender_status_history that doubles as the approval workflow and audit trail.
  • Statuses the blueprint says "will be finalised" are text + CHECK (tune without a migration); stable values (role, category A/E/F, probability H/M/L) are Postgres enums.
06

Roles & permissions

Access stacks two axes: role decides which modules you touch; hierarchy + region decides whose records you see inside them. Customer history is shared; pipeline, tasks and expenses are private to the owner and their manager chain.

MANAGEMENT
Everything, all regions

Lives in dashboards. Approves tender participation. Final authority on performance decisions. Can assign a task to anyone.

REGIONAL MANAGER
One region, full modules

Leads, visits, demos, proposals, tenders, service & performance for their region. 1st-level expense approver; accepts/rejects blockers; does the "also-meet" intervention.

SALES TEAM
Own records, daily driver

Creates & works own leads, logs interactions, plans visits, requests demos/proposals, raises service complaints, own tasks & expenses. Approves nothing.

TENDER TEAM
Owns the tender module

Identifies, prepares, submits, tracks results, logs win/loss. Cannot self-approve participation — pushes to Management.

DEMO TEAM
Demos & equipment

Assigns team, allocates/reserves equipment, confirms dates, records outcomes. Equipment owner approves reservations.

SERVICE TEAM
Tickets end-to-end

Receives complaints, schedules visits, records the service report, closes tickets. Maintains equipment condition.

ACCOUNTS TEAM
Money-out side

2nd-level approver: verifies manager-approved expenses and marks processed (→ Tally). Sees all expenses + expense reports.

ADMIN
System superuser

Creates/invites the 300 users, sets role + manager + region, maintains masters, sees audit log. Not a business viewer.

Don't do it by hand. Build a CSV bulk-invite (email, name, role, manager, region) and run it once; the Contact List import can share the same plumbing. Keep one role per user for the pilot — swap to a user_roles join table in Phase 2 only if true multi-role is needed.

Building module by module

The rule that saves the project: build down, not wide. Take one feature all the way through the stack first — login → list → detail → status change → reminder. Solve the hard parts once, then every other module is copy-rename.

How to read any module from the blueprint

Each module section contains the same four ingredients. Hunt for these four lists and drop each into its slot:

Proof of architecture: when you log in, see 30 real tenders, filter by zone, open one, move it from "under preparation" to "submitted," and the ones closing within 7 days glow red — you've validated the whole stack. Leads, Visits and the Dashboard are then the same moves with different fields, ~3× faster.

The 8-day plan

Day 0 is setup; the tender slice on Day 2 proves everything; never skip the Day-8 buffer.

Day 0
Setup (½ day)

Sign-off on the Phase-1 split. Create Supabase/Vercel/Railway/GitHub. Run the schema. kysely-codegen for types. Deploy the empty apps.

Day 1
Auth + users + real data

JWT guard + @Roles(). User CRUD. Login page + app shell with role-based nav. Import Contact List → organisations/contacts and 30 tenders → tenders.

Day 2
Tender slice, top to bottom

Tender API (list/detail/create/status, guarded + audited). Data table with red deadline badges, detail page, form, role-gated status control. First nightly deadline notification.

Day 3
Leads & timeline

Import Funnel + Bill&Book. Pipeline list, filters, create/edit, status. Interaction timeline on the org.

Day 4
Tender reporting + visits

PQ vs General/MHA counts (§27), win/loss. Visit planning: weekly plan, assign, post-visit update, manager "also-meet."

Day 5
Management dashboard

Exception view: tenders closing in 7 days, follow-ups overdue, pending approvals, region-wise pipeline. Notifications + bell badge.

Day 6
Polish

UI consistency, mobile field layouts, role routing, empty/loading states, audit logging on key actions, Cmd+K.

Day 7
Ship

Bug-bash, deploy to production, load real data, bulk-invite the 300 users, record a short demo video.

Day 8
Buffer

Client walkthrough + fixes. Never skip this.

10

Frontend & design refs

Don't design from scratch — you don't have the days. The 2026 bar for premium B2B dashboards is set by Linear, Attio, Stripe and HubSpot, and the recipe is consistent.

Design system

  • Next.js + Tailwind + shadcn/ui + TypeScript — the stack every premium admin template ships on; shadcn gives production-grade tables, dialogs, forms and command menus you style to look bespoke.
  • Consider buying a shadcn-based admin kit (e.g. TailAdmin, ~$50) to skip 3 days of layout — worth it on a ₹10L project.

What makes it look expensive

  • Role-based home screen — management, regional manager and salesperson each land on a different dashboard showing only what they act on (Attio/Linear "operating surface," not a wall of charts).
  • Restraint — one spacing scale, one type ramp, one accent, lots of whitespace. This alone separates "₹10L" from "template."
  • Dense tables done well (Linear) — fast, filterable, sortable, inline status pills.
  • Command palette (Cmd+K) to jump to any tender/customer — cheap with shadcn, looks premium in a demo.
  • Real empty states so light Phase-2 modules feel intentional, not broken.
  • Mobile-first for field staff (§48 is a functional requirement) — big tap targets, dropdowns over typing, one-click updates.
